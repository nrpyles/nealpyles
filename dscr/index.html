01 / Understand DSCR

Let the property’s income
do the talking.

DSCR stands for Debt Service Coverage Ratio. For many residential rental loans, lenders compare qualifying rent with the property’s full monthly housing payment. Your credit, assets, and the property still matter.

Qualifying monthly rent ÷ Monthly PITIA = DSCR

PITIA means principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and association dues.

1.00 DSCR

Qualifying rent equals the monthly PITIA payment.

1.20 DSCR

Qualifying rent is 20% above monthly PITIA.

Below 1.00

Qualifying rent falls short of the monthly payment.

Commercial properties

Larger commercial properties may use net operating income and a different DSCR method.

A simple monthly example
Principal and interest$1,800
Property taxes$450
Property insurance$150
Association dues$100
Total monthly PITIA$2,500
$3,000 rent ÷ $2,500 PITIA = 1.20 DSCR

Coverage isn’t profit

The $500 difference is before repairs, vacancy, management, utilities you pay, and other operating costs. Budget for those separately.

02 / Qualification and cash

What it takes
to qualify.

These are planning ranges. The selected lender’s program determines your actual terms.

Down payment

Often 20% to 25%. Some scenarios need more equity. Loan to value, or LTV, is the loan amount divided by the property value used by the lender.

Credit profile

660+ is a common starting point. Select programs consider lower scores. Credit history, property type, and the rest of the file affect eligibility and pricing.

Rental coverage

1.00+ is a common target. Some programs prefer 1.20 or higher. Lower coverage options can require more equity or reserves and different pricing.

Reserves

Eligible cash or assets remaining after closing. Several months of housing payments may be needed. Lower coverage or complex files can require more.

Plan for two different buckets

Hypothetical $300,000 purchase with 20% down
Down payment$60,000
Sample closing costs$9,000
Total spent to close$69,000
Example reserves: 6 × $2,500 PITIA$15,000
Example liquidity needed$84,000

This assumes illustrative closing costs and a hypothetical six month reserve requirement. Reserves stay in eligible accounts. They aren’t an added lender fee.

04 / Qualifying and property

The questions
investors ask.

Do I need tax returns or proof of personal income?

Usually, no. DSCR programs generally qualify the property using rental income instead of pay stubs, tax returns, or a personal debt to income ratio. You’ll still document identity, assets, credit, and property details.

What credit score do I need?

Many programs start around 660 or higher, while select options consider lower scores. A stronger profile can improve pricing and available leverage. There isn’t one minimum for every DSCR lender.

What if DSCR is below 1.00?

There may still be options. Lower coverage programs can require a smaller loan, stronger credit, or more reserves. A bigger down payment may improve coverage by reducing the payment.

Can I qualify as a first time investor?

Some programs allow new investors. First time landlord and first time homebuyer are different classifications, and lenders may treat them differently. I’ll confirm your experience rules.

Can I live in the property or house hack it?

Standard DSCR rental programs are for investment use and generally don’t allow you to occupy a unit. Tell me upfront if you plan to live there so we can identify appropriate financing.

Does the property need a tenant or signed lease?

Not always. The lender may use an existing lease, appraiser supported market rent, or an approved rent analysis. A vacant property needs specific review; projected rent isn’t automatically accepted.

Can I finance an Airbnb or vacation rental?

Select programs allow rentals booked for short stays. The lender may use verified operating history, market rent, or approved projections. Tell me about local rental restrictions and HOA rules early.

What property types are eligible?

Common options include single family rentals, townhomes, condos, and properties with 2 to 4 units. Five or more units generally need a commercial or specialized program. Rural, manufactured, mixed use, and unusual properties need early review.

05 / Ownership, terms, and costs

Know the structure.
Understand the cost.

Do I need an LLC?

Not for every program. Many lenders allow personal ownership or an approved entity, while some states or programs require an entity. We’ll confirm vesting and documentation before closing.

Am I personally responsible if I borrow through an LLC?

Often, yes. Many lenders require owners or principals to personally guarantee repayment even when the LLC holds title. Entity ownership doesn’t automatically remove loan liability.

Can I keep buying if I already own several rentals?

Often, yes. DSCR can help finance additional rentals without a traditional personal income calculation. Lenders can limit total exposure, loan count, and reserves. I’ll ask how many financed properties you own.

Are reserves the same as my down payment?

No. Reserves are eligible assets available after closing. Six months of a $2,500 housing payment equals $15,000. The lender sets the actual amount and which accounts count.

What fees should I expect?

Ask for an itemized estimate. Costs can include origination or lender charges, appraisal, title and settlement, recording, prepaid interest, insurance, and initial escrow deposits. Down payment and remaining reserves are separate.

What determines my rate?

Credit, DSCR, loan amount, equity, property type, loan terms, and market conditions affect pricing. Compare options with the same assumptions and hold period. I’ll review your scenario before quoting current terms.

Can I get a fixed rate or interest only payments?

Options may include a fixed rate, adjustable rate, or interest only period. Interest only payments don’t reduce principal during that period, and payments can increase afterward. We’ll review the full payment schedule.

What’s APR? Is it the same as my interest rate?

APR expresses borrowing cost as an annual percentage including the rate and certain finance charges. It isn’t your note rate or payment rate. Business purpose loans can have different disclosure requirements, so compare written fees and terms too.

06 / Refinancing and closing

Your exit plan
matters, too.

Can I pull equity out with a DSCR refinance?

Eligible properties may qualify for a cash out refinance. Proceeds depend on appraised value, maximum LTV, rental coverage, existing liens, costs, and lender limits. Equity alone doesn’t determine the amount.

Can I refinance right after a purchase or renovation?

Possibly. Ownership and loan seasoning rules vary, and lenders may limit which value can be used. For a BRRRR strategy, we’ll review timing, completed improvements, rent support, and cash out rules before you rely on a refinance.

What’s a prepayment penalty?

A charge that can apply if you pay off the loan or make certain large principal payments during a stated period. Some options decrease over time. Shorter periods or no penalty may change pricing and availability.

Can I sell or refinance before the penalty period ends?

You may be able to, but it could trigger the penalty. We’ll review exact triggers, exceptions, expiration, and calculation in your loan documents so the potential cost fits your exit plan.

Can I waive escrows? Does that improve DSCR?

An escrow waiver is lender and scenario specific. If allowed, you pay taxes and insurance directly. Those expenses still count in residential DSCR, so a waiver doesn’t remove them from the calculation.

Can a DSCR loan pay for renovations?

Dedicated renovation or rehab options may be available. Tell me about scope and condition upfront so we can compare the right program and any later refinance. An ordinary rental loan doesn’t automatically fund renovations.

Can we talk numbers before a credit report is ordered?

Yes. We can start with property details and your estimated score. I’ll explain the lender’s credit process and obtain authorization before a report is ordered. Final approval still requires credit review.

How long does closing take?

Crestmark describes a typical timeline of about 3 to 5 weeks for a complete application. Appraisal, title, insurance, entity documents, and underwriting conditions affect timing. We’ll set expectations for your file instead of promising a universal deadline.

Tell me your hold period

If you expect to sell, refinance, or pay down principal soon, we’ll compare exit costs early.
